MONTHLY COMMENTARY (Source: Amundi)
Management commentary

 In October, financial markets moved in an environment marked by the return of inflationary and geopolitical tensions, as well as doubts about the profitability of investments in
 artificial intelligence. The sharp rise in energy prices, fueled by the escalation of the conflict between the United States and Iran and disruptions around the Strait of Hormuz,
 revived fears about the inflation trajectory and reinforced central banks’ caution. While the US economy remains robust and India continues to stand out for its dynamism,
 European activity shows signs of slowing, with a growing divergence between eurozone countries. In the markets, interest rates fell sharply, while fiscal and political tensions
 in France led to a significant widening of the OAT-Bund spread. Global equity markets declined, mainly penalized by the correction in technology and Chinese stocks, while
 the dollar strengthened and oil prices surged.


 UNITED STATES
 In October, the US economy continued its momentum and confirmed its dynamism. Growth remains solid: Q2 GDP was revised to +2.2% annualized (versus +1.5% in the
 initial estimate), while Q3 GDP surprised on the upside at +3.4% annualized versus +3.2% expected. Prospects remain very favorable for businesses. The S&P flash
 manufacturing PMI rose from 55.9 in September to 56, its highest level since May 2022, well above the expansion threshold of 50. This momentum is notably supported by
 AI-related investments and the continuation of an expansionary fiscal policy. However, the labor market has slowed: the unemployment rate rose from 4.1% to 4.2%, and
 annual wage growth decelerated to +3.0%, its lowest level since May 2021 (September data). Consumption remains resilient despite deteriorating purchasing power, with
 retail sales up 0.8% for the month (September data). Inflation, meanwhile, surprised on the upside: September CPI accelerated to +3.7% year-on-year, versus +3.4% in
 August and +3.6% expected, mainly due to rising energy prices. Core inflation, excluding food and energy, rose to +2.6% (versus +2.4% the previous month). PCE inflation,
 the Federal Reserve’s (Fed) preferred indicator, sends a similar signal at +3.6% year-on-year, while core PCE stands at +3.0%, in line with market expectations. As
 anticipated, the Fed kept its key rates unchanged at its October 28 meeting, but the market now prices in a 75% probability of another 25 basis point (bp) hike in December.
 Finally, the midterm election campaign is coming to an end: polls predict a Democratic victory in the House of Representatives and a status quo in the Senate.

 EUROPE
 EUROZONE
 The eurozone’s growth momentum continues to surprise on the upside, particularly in the manufacturing sector, while services are slowing. Q3 GDP grew by 0.4%, above
 expectations; however, this preliminary figure was skewed by the volatility of Irish data. Excluding Ireland, growth is only 0.2%, indicating moderate momentum amid rising
 energy prices and political uncertainty in several countries, including France. October flash PMIs confirm continued expansion, but at a less robust pace than in September.
 The flash composite PMI stands at 51.9, versus 53.1 in September. Manufacturing output remains dynamic (54.2), while services slow to 51.7. In Germany, the flash
 composite PMI fell from 53.8 to 52.8, but remains in expansion for the fourth consecutive month. In France, it dropped to 48.4, signaling a contraction in private sector
 activity; in services, the indicator also fell to 48.4, amid a climate of marked political uncertainty.
 Inflation accelerated: the harmonized consumer price index (HICP) reached +3.8% year-on-year in September, versus +3.2% in August, its highest level in three years. Core
 inflation stands at +2.5%, in line with expectations but still above the European Central Bank’s (ECB) target. This acceleration is mainly explained by the rise in energy prices,
 up +18.8% year-on-year in September, versus +14.3% in August. The ECB raised its deposit rate by 25 bp to 2.50% in September; it maintained the status quo at its October
 29 meeting, in line with market expectations.

 UNITED KINGDOM
 UK activity data for October indicate that the economy retains solid momentum. The S&P manufacturing PMI came in at 52.8, in line with market forecasts. However,
 consumer confidence is deteriorating sharply: the GfK index measuring household sentiment fell from -13 to -24 in October, its steepest monthly drop since the pandemic.
 Inflation accelerated sharply in September, with headline CPI reaching 3.5% year-on-year, an 11-month high and above the consensus of 3.4%, mainly due to a surge in
 automotive fuel costs. Core inflation remained at 2.8% year-on-year, unchanged from the previous month. The Bank of England kept its key rate at 3.75%, but a hike is widely
 anticipated at the November 5 meeting due to the high level of inflation.

 JAPAN
 The Japanese economy continues to surprise positively. The manufacturing sector continued its expansion in October: the S&P manufacturing PMI stood at 53.3, down from
 September (54.1), but still in expansion territory. This indicator reflects sustained production growth, driven by dynamic exports. Exports rose 16.7% in value year-on-year in
 September, marking a thirteenth consecutive month of increase. The weakness of the yen (USD/JPY near 158) is a supportive factor for export-oriented industries. Politically,
 Finance Minister Satsuki Katayama has nevertheless renewed warnings to currency speculators, reaffirming the possibility of another joint intervention by the United States
 and Japan to stabilize the yen.
 The consumer price index (CPI) for September stands at 2.1% year-on-year, while Tokyo CPI excluding fresh food and energy, an indicator of underlying trend, is at 2.4% in
 October. The Bank of Japan kept its key rate unchanged at 1.25% at its October 30 meeting, while indicating that the country has finally emerged from several decades of
 deflation. Markets anticipate another rate hike in December, following statements by Governor Kazuo Ueda. However, the rise in medium- and long-term interest rates is
 significantly increasing the interest burden and weighing on the Japanese government budget.

 Global equity markets experienced a phase of consolidation, with a decline of -1.7% over the month for the MSCI ACWI USD (+9.9% year-to-date), mainly linked to profit-
 taking on the AI theme. The VIX rose to 19% from 16% at the end of September; the market is doubting both the future profitability of AI investments and is suffering the
 inflationary shock caused by the renewed rise in oil prices, which is forcing central banks to react. Q3 earnings releases were also a source of volatility. Developed markets
 nevertheless outperformed emerging markets: MSCI World (USD) NTR fell by -0.8% (+9.7% year-to-date), while MSCI Emerging Markets suffered a much sharper drop of
 6.6% (+13.3% year-to-date).
 There is strong heterogeneity in performance. In the United States, the Nasdaq 100 fell by -6.7% in October and the S&P 500 declined more modestly by 0.9%, while the
 Dow Jones Industrial Average gained 2.2%, reflecting a rotation from growth and technology stocks to defensive and industrial stocks. In Europe, the Euro Stoxx 50 lost
 -2.2% while the Stoxx Europe 600 rose by 1.9%. The CAC 40 edged up 0.1%, while the DAX 40 recorded a 2.5% increase for the month. In Asia, where the AI theme has
 been a powerful performance driver, consolidation was clear: the Nikkei 225 fell by 5.2% and the TOPIX by 3.5%.
