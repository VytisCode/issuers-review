Robeco Euro Credit Bonds I EUR

Performance commentary
Based on transaction prices, the fund's return was -1.52%.
The portfolio posted a negative return in September, driven by an increase in underlying government bond yields and a widening of credit spreads. Underlying 5-
year yields rose from 3.06% to 3.35%, while 10-year yields moved from 3.32% to 3.58%. Credit spreads widened throughout the month, with an acceleration in
the last week. The average index spread ended the month at 91 basis points, 12 basis points wider than at the end of August. The credit excess return of the
index was -0.37%, while the total return of the index was -1.33%. The portfolio performance was slightly worse than that of the index. The beta positioning of
the portfolio was a little bit above neutral (1), this detracted from the performance as credit spreads widened. Issuer selection had a positive contribution.
Underweights in the consumer non-cyclical and communications sectors contributed positively, as did selection within the BBB rating bucket. The best-
performing individual positions, on a risk-adjusted basis, were Teva, Beignet (structured data center deal with an A+ rating) and Oracle. Detractors from
performance were Volkswagen, TenneT and Verizon (underweight).
Market development
European IG credit spreads widened materially in September, with the Bloomberg corporate index spread moving from 79 bps to 91 bps. The move was broadly
one-directional, reflecting persistent macro headwinds. The ECB hiked by 25 bps to 2.5%, its second increase since the Iran war drove energy prices higher in
February, and markets moved to price in four further hikes over 12 months. A global bond rout, with USD 100 oil stoking inflation fears, amplified the pressure,
as did French deficit worries late in the month. Despite this, September included a record single-day supply of EUR 18 bln on 9 September, driven largely by US
issuers. Government bond returns were negative. 10-year US Treasury yields rose 54 bps to 5.28%, the highest since 2007, while 10-year Bund yields rose 26
bps to 3.59%, a 17-year high. The sell-off was driven by rising energy prices and resilient data, with Eurozone and US PMIs surprising to the upside. The Fed also
raised rates by 25 bps, to 3.75-4.0%. The French 10-year spread over Germany widened by 42 bps to 127 bps, the highest since 2012.
Expectation of fund manager
The balance in credit markets is shifting. The most notable change in our latest quarterly outlook is our assessment of market technicals. As the market moves
from bond scarcity to increasing supply, the primary calendar has become significantly heavier. New deals from data center operators and related issuers are
pressuring outstanding bonds in those sectors and show the first signs of issuance fatigue. Credit spreads widened meaningfully in September, though they
remain low from a longer-term perspective. We remain wary of political risk in Europe, expressed via an underweight in French issuers. While we have yet to see
large-scale outflows, attractive cash yields have reduced the need for investors to move further along the risk spectrum. With governments and corporations
competing for capital, we believe technicals are unlikely to provide the same tailwind as in recent years. Against this backdrop, we remain cautious on credit
markets. Within investment grade, selective opportunities still offer attractive carry without excessive risk. We continue to favor shorter spread duration given
the weakening technical backdrop, despite still-supportive corporate fundamentals.
Top 10 largest holdings
In our portfolio management, the most relevant issuer positions are those measured in risk points (weight x spread x duration). The largest positions
consist of a mix of financials and industrials. Often, we have more than one bond holding in a specific name.
Sector allocation
In our portfolio management, we do not only factor in weights, but also spreads and spread durations (DTS). On that basis, we are overweight in financials
and underweight in non-financial corporates. The banking sector remains an overweight, as balance sheets are strong and profitability is high. The fund
holds an off-benchmark position in covered bonds; spreads for covered bonds are relatively high in a historic perspective and the AAA-rated bonds suit very
well in our defensive beta positioning. The position in ABS consists of European residential mortgages and auto loans. The agencies category comprises
issuers that are majority-owned by governments.
Currency denomination allocation
Almost all of our bond exposures are denominated in euros. Holdings denominated in GBP or USD are hedged back to the euro benchmark by default.
Duration allocation
The policy of the fund is to have a duration position that is neutral against its benchmark.
Rating allocation
We have no clear preference for specific rating buckets. Our positioning over the different rating buckets is the result of beta positioning, sector allocation
and issuer selection.
Subordination allocation
In the allocation to the capital structure, we favor the bonds with solid risk-adjusted performance potential while taking into account the beta, sector
themes, and the credit cycle. The exposure to subordinated bonds that we do have, is limited to positions that have both a good fundamental outlook as
well as a robust bond structure.

Fiscal product treatment

Dividend policy
The fund does not distribute a dividend. The income earned by the fund is reflected in its share price. This means that the fund's total performance is reflected
in its share price performance.
