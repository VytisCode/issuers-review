MONTHLY COMMENTARY (Source: Amundi)
Management commentary

 UNITED STATES
 In September 2026, the US economy confirmed a reacceleration of activity in Q3 driven by investment, particularly related to artificial intelligence. The September flash
 composite PMI reached its highest level since mid-2021, driven by the manufacturing sector. Private sector job creation continued to support the labor market. Inflation
 remained high: August CPI came in at +3.4% YoY (stable), core CPI was +2.4% YoY, in line with expectations. However, the PCE, the Federal Reserve’s (Fed) preferred
 measure of inflation, surprised to the downside in August; headline PCE came in at +3.4% YoY (expected at +3.7%) while core PCE fell to +3.0% YoY (expected at +3.4%), a
 deceleration of 30bps compared to July. On a monthly basis, core PCE rose by only +0.2% versus +0.3% expected. The ongoing standoff with Iran led to sustained fuel
 prices. In the face of persistent inflation, the Federal Reserve raised its rates by +25 basis points (bps) unanimously, marking the first hike since July 2023 and a turning point
 in US monetary policy. Kevin Warsh, the Fed Chair, maintains a resolutely restrictive (hawkish) stance and the market anticipates one to two further rate hikes before the end
 of 2026. Mortgage financing rates have exceeded 7%, which should slow the real estate sector in the coming quarters.

 EUROZONE
 The eurozone surprised positively in September, with leading indicators accelerating and an improvement in economic sentiment. The flash composite PMI rose to 53.1,
 above expectations, driven by a simultaneous acceleration in services (53.0, highest in 10 months) and manufacturing output (53.4, highest in 55 months). Activity held up
 better than expected in the face of rising energy prices, particularly natural gas prices directly linked to the blockade of the Strait of Hormuz. The Ifo survey was a surprise
 and showed that business confidence improved. While activity in Germany and Spain continued to accelerate, it returned to expansion in France, but remains under pressure
 amid a tense national political context. The state of French public finances is worrying investors. The 2026 deficit is expected at 5.4% of GDP (versus 5% initially forecast),
 and debt is expected to exceed 120% of GDP in 2027. SCOPE downgraded France’s rating from AA- to A+. The 10-year OAT-Bund spread reached 127 bps, a 15-year high.
 At the eurozone level, inflation accelerated less than expected; the harmonized consumer price index (HICP) for August was revised down to +3.2% YoY, compared to +3.3%
 in the first estimate. The European Central Bank (ECB) chief economist Philip Lane also warned that high energy prices could keep inflation elevated for longer than expected.
 In this context, the ECB raised its rates as expected by +25bps and indicated it could continue this move. In Germany, the Landtag election in Saxony-Anhalt on September 6
 had a national political impact as the AfD is well ahead and could govern a Land alone for the first time since 1945.

 UNITED KINGDOM
 The United Kingdom presented a stronger-than-expected economic picture in September, with the manufacturing PMI at 51.9, compared to a market forecast of 51.5.
 Inflation picked up again, with August CPI at +3.1% YoY, but core inflation remained at +2.6%. The Bank of England (BoE) kept its rates at 3.75% at its September meeting.
 The BoE warned that high energy prices could start to feed through to wages and purchasing power, and abandoned its plan to sell long-term government bonds, easing
 pressure on the UK bond market.

 JAPAN
 The Japanese economy continues its expansion phase. The PMI confirmed a positive trend for the manufacturing sector in September, standing at 54.1. Manufacturing sector
 growth remained particularly impressive amid a sharp rise in exports, partly due to the weakness of the yen. However, supply chain constraints, often linked to the war in the
 Middle East, have increased already high inflationary pressures. August CPI came in slightly below expectations at +1.9% YoY (versus +2.0% expected), but Tokyo CPI
 excluding fresh food and energy stood at +1.9% in August, confirming a gradual upward trend. In this context, the Bank of Japan (BoJ) raised its policy rate by +25bps to
 1.25%. Governor Kazuo Ueda justified the hike by the need to act preemptively against the risk of inflation overshooting. However, the decision was not unanimous: two
 members of the Monetary Policy Board voted against the rate hike. The rise in medium- and long-term interest rates is significantly increasing the interest burden and
 weighing on the Japanese government budget.

 September was a difficult month for global equity markets, penalized by the simultaneous monetary tightening of major central banks, the rise in long-term rates, and a
 confusing situation in the Middle East. The AI theme remained central but doubts about the profitability of investments weighed on market sentiment. The MSCI ACWI (USD)
 fell by -1.6% over the month, bringing its YTD performance to +11.8%. The MSCI World (USD) NTR posted a similar performance at -1.7% (+10.7% in 2026), illustrating that
 the correction is affecting all developed markets. The MSCI Emerging Markets index held up better in relative terms with -1.2% over the month, although the Indian market
 recorded a sharp decline (the Nifty 50 fell by -6.1%); performance nevertheless remains high since the start of the year at +21.1%. The VIX volatility index rose slightly to
 16%, reflecting growing investor concern over the decline in equity markets. In the United States, the S&P 500 fell -0.8% over the month, while the Nasdaq 100 gained
 +3.3%, supported by continued strong earnings from major tech stocks. The Dow Jones (-2.6% in September) and the Russell 2000 (-3.4%) suffered more in this high long-
 term rate environment, with small caps (small caps) being particularly sensitive to the cost of credit.
 European equities underperformed. The Eurostoxx 50 fell by -3.3% over the month and the broad Stoxx Europe 600 index fell by -3.1%, although the latter still posted a
 +7.2% gain for the year. France underperformed significantly: the CAC 40 dropped -5.2% over the month, penalized by the crisis of confidence among investors regarding
 French public finances and political uncertainty surrounding the budget vote. The DAX also fell by -5.2% over the month despite an improvement in economic prospects in
 Germany.
 In Asia, the Nikkei 225 rose by +0.5% despite the BoJ rate hike, supported by strong Japanese exports favored by the persistent weakness of the yen. The TOPIX, however,
 lost -0.9%.
