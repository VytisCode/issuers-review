General Principles for Preparing the Sustainability Report

1 General Principles for Preparing
the Sustainability Report
The sustainability report of AS Tallinna Sadam (hereinafter also Tallinna Sadam, the Company or the
Group) has been prepared for the financial year covering the period from 1 January to 31 December
2025. The report reflects the Group's sustainability-related activities, performance, and impacts
in the environmental, social, and governance areas (hereinafter also ESG) during the reporting period.
The sustainability report has not been prepared to the mandatory extent of the requirements
of the Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive CSRD1 of the European Union, nor does it fully
comply with the requirements of the European Sustainability Reporting Standards ESRS2. However,
the report has been prepared following the principles of the CSRD and ESRS and is based on the
framework set out in the ESRS. Due to the legal status of the Company and the applicable reporting
requirements, not all disclosure requirements arising from the directive and standards are applicable
and mandatory for Tallinna Sadam; therefore, this report does not include all data points and disclosure
obligations defined in the ESRSs.
The report includes the 2025 consolidated sustainability data of Tallinna Sadam's subsidiaries. Data
for the associate Green Marine AS has not been included in the sustainability report, as Tallinna Sadam
lacks a controlling influence over the company, despite a 51% shareholding.
In assessing sustainability activities and impacts, in addition to the indicators set out in the ESRSs,
company-specific metrics, developed to monitor and evaluate the achievement of Tallinna Sadam's
strategic environmental, social and business objectives, are also used.
The sustainability report has been prepared voluntarily with the aim of increasing transparency, supporting responsible and sustainable management and meeting the expectations of the owner and
stakeholders.
The sustainability report primarily covers topics and data points that, as a result of the double materiality assessment, have been assessed as material to Tallinna Sadam. Additionally, certain topics
whose impact was not assessed as material in the double materiality assessment, but which Tallinna
Sadam considers important for informing stakeholders and ensuring a comprehensive overview, have
also been disclosed.

2 Sustainable Development Strategy
The sustainable development strategy focuses on how sustainability considerations are systematically
integrated into Tallinna Sadam's strategy, business model, and value chain. The Group's sustainability
objectives are aligned with the overall strategy and reflect the integration of key ESG3 impact areas
into the strategic framework. Such alignment ensures that the Group meets regulatory requirements
and stakeholder expectations and creates long-term value through sustainable management
and operating principles. More environmentally friendly operations and the systematic handling
of environmental issues increase the competitiveness of Tallinna Sadam. The Group implements
energy-efficient technologies and green innovations, which have helped improve resource efficiency.
In this way, economic performance has been aligned with environmental responsibility.

especially the Baltic Sea ecosystem. Cooperation is carried out with local governments, research
institutions, and the largest ports of the Baltic Sea. Sustainability is a principle that guides the organisation to act responsibly both today and in the future.

The Group's value chain reflects the results of the double materiality assessment, during which
the main stages where the Group's activities have the greatest environmental, social, and
economic impact were identified. The results of the analysis were used to integrate sustainability
considerations into the Group's strategic framework, thereby ensuring the management of
sustainability-related financial risks and opportunities, as well as responsibility towards stakeholders and the environment.

The Company's goal for sustainable development is to contribute to achieving climate neutrality,
increasing energy efficiency, and promoting the circular economy. Protecting the Baltic Sea as
a sensitive ecosystem and maintaining a clean urban space are of key importance, given that
Tallinna Sadam's activities are located in densely populated areas. An important role is also played
by the provision of the Ferry service, which ensures the connection between Estonia's main islands
and the mainland, as well as by innovation and responsible business and development activities.

In its business and development activities, Tallinna Sadam has consistently contributed to reducing negative environmental impacts, while bearing responsibility for the natural ecosystem, and

Through its activities, Tallinna Sadam contributes to the fulfilment of Estonia's national climate goals,
the European Union Green Deal, and the UN Sustainable Development Goals.

Tallinna Sadam aims to be a reliable partner for customers and investors, and an attractive employer

the organisation's activities, and one of its most important directions is the management of environmental impacts.

Management of Sustainable Development

3 Management of Sustainable Development
Goals are managed and monitored at the management level, involving experts from various fields,
including energy, environment, waste management, safety, personnel, and communications.
In addition to internal activities, partners, customers, suppliers, and other stakeholders are also
involved in achieving goals and value-based operations.
Sustainable development is an organic part of Tallinna Sadam's management. Strategic objectives
serve as the basis for evaluating business projects, making management decisions, and in the daily
activities of employees. To achieve the sustainable development goals, core activities and action
plans have been defined and are monitored using a system of metrics with target levels developed
in 2020 and updated during the double materiality assessment in 2024–2025.
The ESG action plans are managed by the Group's ESG working group, which includes the Head
of the Environmental Management Department, the Head of the Marketing and Communication
Department, and the Investor Relations Manager. The working group is responsible for the development, supervision, and reporting of action plans in the E, S, and G areas. Additionally, the Group has
an internal audit department whose task is to provide an independent and impartial assessment
of the effectiveness of the Group's risk management and processes. Follow-up audits are performed
regularly to verify that the risks identified in the risk assessment report are updated and mitigated.
The Group cooperates with external experts and consultants when additional or expert knowledge
is required in certain areas. In 2025, external support was used from the consulting firm Civitta Eesti
AS in the double materiality assessment process, climate risk assessment, and ESG data analysis.
Experts from the TalTech Estonian Maritime Academy were also involved in the data collection and

calculation processes for Scope 1, 2, and 3 greenhouse gas (hereinafter also GHG) emissions.
As a rule, experts for advisory services are selected through tenders, where a significant criterion
is the provision of advisory services by providers with experience in the relevant field. Methodologies
for collecting data and KPIs are developed and implemented in cooperation with internal financial
and subject-matter experts to ensure accuracy, consistency, and data quality.
Looking ahead, all major business development and growth projects are linked to sustainable
development themes and support the achievement of set goals and the shaping of a human-friendly
natural environment in the Baltic Sea region.
To implement the sustainable development strategy, annual goals are set, to which all employees
of the company contribute. The management of Tallinna Sadam is based on the principle that daily
business decisions and investments must support the development of both the company and the
surrounding environment and society.
In 2025, Tallinna Sadam received the ‘Rohevõsu 2025’ recognition, earning third place in the green
companies ranking as part of the Estonia's TOP 1000 successful companies assessment compiled
by Äripäev. Eesti Pakendiringlus, which established the green company award, will plant 250 trees
on behalf of Tallinna Sadam. The green companies ranking was compiled using an ESG assessment
methodology based on the sustainability principles highlighted in the reports of Estonia's TOP 100
most successful companies. All participating companies were analysed based on a uniform model
and their ESG level was calculated.

Double Materiality Assessment

4 Double Materiality Assessment
From 2024–2025, we conducted a double materiality (impacts, risks, and opportunities, i.e. IRO)
assessment in accordance with the European Sustainability Reporting Standard (ESRS). The aim
of the assessment was to define material topics, taking into account both the organisation's impact
on the environment and society and the potential impact of these impacts, risks, and opportunities
on the company's financial position. The assessment took place in workshops for each business area
of Tallinna Sadam — passengers, cargo, shipping, and real estate — involving specialists from
the respective fields. The workshops were led by sustainability experts from Civitta Eesti AS, who
supported the process with methodology, the assessment framework, and discussion management.

In addition, for each topic, it was noted whether it directly affects the company, entities operating
in its value chain, or both. An impact is considered actual if it has already occurred, and potential
if there is a probability of its occurrence. In the assessment or scoring, scales for evaluating the
criteria were agreed upon with the Tallinna Sadam ESG working group, based on the company's
risk management and environmental aspect assessment procedures. In the assessment of impact
materiality, the criteria used were the scale, scope, irremediability, and likelihood of the impact;
in the case of financial materiality, the scale and likelihood were assessed. All assessments were
given on a uniform five-point scale.

The first stage of the double materiality assessment involved compiling an initial list of sustainabilityrelated topics. This was based on ESRS 1 Appendix A (the ‘long list’), an analysis of the company's
business activities, relevant industry reports, existing sustainability reporting standards and guidelines, and reports from companies operating in similar sectors.

After assessment based on the criteria, the values obtained were added up, resulting in a score
for each risk or opportunity. Subsequently, an arithmetic mean was calculated for all relevant
impacts, risks, and opportunities (separately for positive and negative impacts), which was used
as the materiality threshold. Aggregate scores that exceeded the arithmetic mean of the respective
group were classified as material.

Based on the list of topics obtained, a catalogue of impacts, risks, and opportunities was compiled,
reflecting both actual and potential, positive and negative aspects. The company's management,
selected stakeholders, and an independent consulting partner were involved in the assessment,
helping to evaluate the relevance of the topics in the context of the company's activities and value
chain.
As a result of the assessment, impact-material topics (positive and negative) and financially material
topics (risks and opportunities) relevant to the business lines were mapped in accordance with ESRS
requirements. The compiled list with the corresponding topics was structured into a materiality
assessment form, which allowed topics to be assessed based on predefined criteria, depending
on the nature of the company's activities and materiality factors.
For the double materiality assessment, the establishment of key performance indicators, action
plans, and this report, the following time horizons have been defined:
• Short-term: 0–1 years (reporting year)
• Medium-term: 1–5 years
• Long-term: 5+ years

By consolidating all impacts, risks, and opportunities across business lines that exceeded the materiality threshold, the results were aggregated into Tallinna Sadam's double materiality matrix (Figure
2) of impacts, risks, and opportunities (Impacts, Risks, and Opportunities (hereinafter IROs)).
Since the IROs derived from the topics included in the long list were assessed separately for each
business area (passenger transport, cargo transport, shipping, and real estate), a topic is considered
doubly material for the Tallinna Sadam group even if it is material in only one business area.
The matrix presents Tallinna Sadam's IROs, grouped according to ESRS areas. A detailed description
of the IROs and their connection to ESRS topics is provided in the respective E, S, and G chapters.

5 Stakeholder Engagement
in the Double Materiality Assessment
In order for Tallinna Sadam to properly assess its impact on the environment and society and to
identify material risks and opportunities, it is important to actively involve stakeholders associated
with all business lines. Feedback from stakeholders provides additional input that supports the identification of material reportable topics and subsequent reporting.
The impacts, risks, and opportunities (IROs) identified based on stakeholder assessments and
the results of the company's internal analysis form the basis of Tallinna Sadam's sustainable
development strategy and define the topics on which the company focuses in its management
and reporting.
Tallinna Sadam's main stakeholders to be involved were identified through value chain mapping,
based on the stakeholders' potential impact and importance to Tallinna Sadam's operations
(e.g. stakeholders accounting for a large share of the company's total revenue or playing
an important role in the long term) and Tallinna Sadam's impact on stakeholders (e.g. suppliers
accounting for a large share of the company's cost base). The parties in the value chain were
mapped taking into account Tallinna Sadam's main business lines and the size of the companies,
which also served as the basis for the double materiality assessment process. The parties
included:
• Group employees;
• Tallinna Sadam's customers and end-users of services (including travel operators,
agents, terminal operators, developers, and tenants);
• specific customers and suppliers of TS Laevad and TS Shipping;
• Group-wide stakeholders (including investors and financiers, industrial parks,
government agencies);
• Suppliers (including construction companies, support, and utility service providers).

After mapping Tallinna Sadam's value chain and defining the stakeholders, a web-based survey was
selected as the engagement channel. Although feedback is collected annually through employee and
customer satisfaction surveys, direct communication from business line managers and administrative
managers, and community events, which allow for more frequent ongoing feedback than an annual
sustainability questionnaire, the ESG working group decided to conduct a separate survey covering
sustainability topics and to assess the importance of sustainability themes annually in customer
satisfaction surveys. The questionnaire for own employees was sent to all company employees
working in the Group's parent company and the subsidiaries TS Laevad OÜ and TS Shipping OÜ.
The purpose of stakeholder engagement was to assess the objectivity of the double materiality
assessment and, if necessary, to take stakeholder evaluations into account when assessing impact
and financial materiality. Stakeholders were asked to provide assessments of Tallinna Sadam’s
environmental, social, and governance areas on a seven-point scale (1 – not aware; 7 – Tallinna
Sadam's activity is significant), which allowed for the highlighting of areas and topics considered
most important. Based on stakeholder feedback, topics directly related to Tallinna Sadam's core
activities and its role in public service emerged as the most significant.
Based on the results of the employee survey, the most important environmental topics for Tallinna
Sadam are the development of the circular economy and waste management, as well as the prevention of marine pollution. In the social area, employees consider the most important topics to be
ensuring safety for employees, passengers, and customers, as well as accessibility to services, i.e.
the provision of vital services, including ensuring vessel traffic and the exchange of goods in harbours and between Estonia's main islands. In the area of business governance, employees most
value Tallinna Sadam's innovation and digitalisation activities and their purposeful management,
as well as the assessment, mitigation, and management of risks related to the entire Group.

6.1 Climate risk analysis
A climate risk assessment was conducted for the Tallinna Sadam Group using the TCFD (Task Force
on Climate-related Financial Disclosures) framework. The assessment was carried out across the Group
and by business lines in cooperation with the consulting firm Civitta Eesti AS, following a similar logic
to the double materiality assessment.
Climate-related impacts, risks, and opportunities were already identified as material during the double
materiality assessment, which is why they were further addressed in a separate climate risk analysis.
The focus was on:
• physical risks arising from temperature changes, increased frequency of storms,
increased precipitation, and fluctuations in water levels, etc.;
• transition risks, which include regulatory changes, technological developments, changes
in market and consumer behaviour (reputational risk), supply chain vulnerability, etc.

Figure 6. Climate risk assessment criteria
ASSESSED CLIMATE SCENARIOS:
Prevention of global warming (target 1.5°C) — RCP 1.9
Gradual transition to a low-emission economy,
with the average temperature increase not exceeding 2°C by 2100.

Low global warming (up to 2°C) — RCP 2.6
Late transition to a low-emission economy, rapid regulatory changes,
with the average temperature increase of up to 2°C by 2100.

In the analysis, we used Copernicus climate maps, IPCC WGI Atlas data, and Estonian future climate
scenarios up to the year 2100 as input. In addition, the working groups assessed impacts more
broadly based on cognitive experience. Transition risks were assessed in the Estonian and EU context,

6.2.3 Policies, measures, and targets
Tallinna Sadam treats climate change as both a strategic risk and an opportunity to develop carbonfree, innovative, and forward-looking business models. The topic of climate change was assessed
as material during the double materiality analysis, which sets a clear expectation for Tallinna Sadam
to develop GHG emission reduction measures and an action plan. In shaping climate policy, European
Union climate goals are taken into account, including the ‘Fit for 55’ package, under which the EU
has committed to reducing greenhouse gas emissions by at least 55% by 2030 compared to 1990
levels.
Tallinna Sadam applies a proactive approach, identifying environmental and climate risks early and
planning activities to prevent or mitigate their impact. At the heart of climate policy is a gradual
transition to renewable energy sources and the reduction of greenhouse gas emissions along
the entire value chain. The climate policy is closely integrated with Tallinna Sadam's sustainable
development strategy and is reflected in investment decisions and action plans.
Tallinna Sadam's long-term goal is to achieve climate neutrality for the company's operations
and zero emissions for vessels stationary at the port by 2050. At the same time, Tallinna Sadam
monitors opportunities for the sustainable consumption of natural resources and has set a goal
of increasing energy efficiency and using renewable energy for 90% of its own energy consumption.

Development of alternative fuels and green energy
As a significant measure, Tallinna Sadam is planning an alternative fuel HUB in Muuga and Paldiski
South Harbour, which will create the prerequisites for the transition of shipping and transport
to more environmentally friendly fuels. The HUB includes the production, storage, and bunkering
of alternative fuels and supports the growing need of both maritime transport and industry
for sustainable energy solutions.

In Muuga Harbour, JetGas Eesti plans to build a liquefied methane terminal, where five tanks
with a total capacity of 5,000 m3 are planned. The terminal will primarily enable the procurement
and bunkering of bioLNG, but also other methane fuels. The terminal is planned to become fully
operational in 2030.
Green ammonia production is being developed in Estonia by the company Derivaat NH3, which plans
to establish a green ammonia plant in Paldiski South Harbour. Derivaat NH3 aims to produce up
to 20,000 tonnes of green ammonia per year, with the start of production planned for 2028.
Renewable energy production and storage is a new business and growth area for Tallinna Sadam,
supporting the company's long-term sustainability goals. Increasing the availability of alternative
fuels allows for the reduction of greenhouse gas emissions, improvement of air quality, and moving
step-by-step towards achieving climate neutrality and zero emissions for vessels stationary
at the port by 2050.

Servicing of fully electric and hybrid vessels
To accommodate and serve fully electric and hybrid vessels, Tallinna Sadam is developing the electricity infrastructure in Old City Harbour. The 110/10 kV substation building complex to be constructed in Old City Harbour will be located in the immediate vicinity of the cruise terminal. The completion
of the substation is planned for 2028. The electricity infrastructure is a prerequisite for the introduction of fully electric ro-pax type passenger ships on the Tallinn–Helsinki route, where Viking Line
plans to introduce a fully electric passenger ship specifically designed for the Tallinn–Helsinki route
starting from 2030.
In Muuga Harbour, the construction of the onshore power supply (OPS) connection for vessels is
underway; the quayside power supply for ro-pax and container ships is planned to be completed
by the end of 2027 as part of the TwinPort VI project co-financed by the European Union.

6.2.4 Energy consumption and energy efficiency
Since 2021, the parent company Tallinna Sadam has been purchasing only renewable electricity for
its own use. In 2025, the company's purchase of renewable electricity was 12.3 GWh. In addition, we
produced 5.2% of our electricity consumption using solar panels in Old City Harbour, Muuga Harbour,
and Paldiski South Harbour. The Old City Harbour cruise terminal building is heated and cooled by
seawater, and the output of the terminal's solar panels covered 54% of the cruise terminal's electricity consumption. Total energy consumption (heat and electricity) was 16.8 GWh, of which the share
of renewable energy was 77%.
During the navigation of the Botnica, the principles of the SEEMP (Ship Energy Efficiency Management
Plan) are followed, including the optimisation of the vessel's course and speed with the aim of avoiding strong headwinds and swell (weather routing). This approach has made it possible to reduce fuel
consumption and ensure safer ocean crossings. In 2025, icebreaking services were provided in Estonian
waters, and the vessel participated in a rescue operation in Northern Canada.
Since 2024, TS Laevad has been purchasing electricity only from renewable sources for its fleet
and office premises. The ferries of TS Laevad — Piret, Tõll, Leiger, and Tiiu — are equipped with the
innovative Blueflow Energy Management fuel consumption monitoring system, which helps improve
navigators' driving techniques in real time, use optimal speed according to the navigation area, and

plan the necessary cleaning of the ship's underwater hull to save fuel, thereby reducing the negative
environmental impact resulting from marine fuel. For energy efficiency purposes, the replacement of
lighting with LED technology continued on the vessels, digital logbooks were installed, and the ventilation system was optimised. Compared to the base year 2019, when the monitoring system was not
yet implemented, the average fuel consumption of the vessels per scheduled trip in 2025 was 15%
lower (2024: 11%). In 2025, the GHG emissions of TS Laevad decreased not only due to the fuel
consumption monitoring system but also in connection with the consumption of electricity from renewable energy sources, resulting in GHG emissions per scheduled trip for TS Laevad being 3% lower
in 2025 than in 2024. In cooperation with the onshore power supply (OPS) equipment manufacturer
Shore Link OÜ, TS Laevad tested onshore power supply (OPS) capabilities over a two-week period.
As part of this, the ferry Piret was connected to the onshore power supply (OPS) equipment installed
quayside at Virtsu Harbour. The purpose of the test is to evaluate the technical and operational possibilities for connecting ferries to a land-based power source, which in the future would allow for
the charging of electric vessel batteries while the vessel is stationary at the quay. As a result of
the test, changes were made to the charging system at Saarte Liinid's harbours, and the tests are
planned to continue.

6.2.5 Scope 1-3 GHG emissions and total GHG emissions

Changes in Scope 3 (visiting vessels) calculations

GHG emissions associated with Tallinna Sadam's activities are calculated based on the 2006
guidelines6 of the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC). The Company has been
measuring its emissions since 2019, based on the ownership or control of the emission source
across different scopes7:
• Scope 1 — Direct emission sources of Tallinna Sadam: vessels, vehicles, other equipment,
and boiler houses owned by Tallinna Sadam.
• Scope 2 — Indirect emission sources of Tallinna Sadam: purchased electricity and thermal
energy for buildings and infrastructure owned by Tallinna Sadam.
• Scope 3 — Other indirect emission sources: tenants, operators, vessels visiting the port,
traffic passing through the port and ro-ro cargo, cargo handling equipment, and railway
traffic.

Until 2023, monitoring and reporting of only CO2 emissions from visiting vessels were required under
International Maritime Organization (IMO) and EU-level regulations. However, as of 2024, the EU has
expanded reporting requirements to include emissions of other greenhouse gases.

As the major impacts directly related to Tallinna Sadam's GHG emissions (Scope 1-2) are fuel for
ferries, electricity and heat consumption, and the choice of transport vehicles, energy efficiency and
the increased use of renewable energy sources are the key drivers for GHG reduction. More than half
of total GHG emissions (Scope 1-3) consist of GHG emissions from vessels visiting our harbours, the
reduction of which we can only influence indirectly by creating environmentally friendly port services
and encouraging their use.
The total GHG emissions resulting from Tallinna Sadam's activities in a broader scope, i.e. including
operators, tenants, and vessel calls (Scope 1, 2, and 3 combined), were 57,034 tonnes of CO2
in 2024, which is a 54% decrease compared to 2019 (base year) (2023: 72,279 tonnes of CO2),
primarily due to the higher use of electricity from renewable energy sources in 2024 and a decrease
in emissions related to vessel calls.

Changes have occurred in EMSA8 reporting since 2024 due to the amendment of the MRV (Monitoring,
Reporting and Verification) Regulation, which added other greenhouse gases to CO₂. The MRV
Regulation (EU 2015/757) regulates the monitoring, reporting, and verification of greenhouse gas
emissions from ships. Although only CO₂ emissions are still considered within the EU ETS for the
years 2024–2025, EMSA reporting now also reflects CO₂ equivalent, which is higher than actual
CO₂ in tonnes, as it includes methane and nitrous oxide emissions in addition to CO₂.
Tallinna Sadam's own direct and indirect GHG emissions (Scope 1 and 2 combined) were 19,261
tonnes of CO2 in 2025 (21,597 tonnes of CO2 in 2024). Of this, 75% is related to the operation
of the Ferry service between the Estonian mainland and the main islands. Compared to the previous
year, the Group's CO2 emissions decreased by approximately 11% (a 42% decrease compared
to the 2019 base year). The decrease in emissions was mainly due to the lower fuel consumption
of the Botnica in connection with the reduction in the vessel's charter time in 2025 and maintenance
work; emissions also decreased due to TS Laevad's transition to electricity produced from renewable
energy sources and the lower fuel consumption of ferries achieved through energy efficiency measures. As the number of scheduled trips by TS Laevad was 0.6% lower compared to 2024, CO2 emissions per scheduled trip in 2025 were 3% lower than in 2024 (16% lower than in 2019). The main
reason for the decrease was the mild winter and light ice conditions in 2025, in contrast to the heavy
ice in the winter of 2024, which resulted in significantly higher fuel consumption (and thus emissions) for the vessels.

6.3.2 Policies, measures, and targets

Marine pollution has been identified as a negative impact and environmental risk for the company.
Negative impacts may include water pollution, primarily during vessel bunkering and the loading
and unloading of hazardous cargo, during which pollutants may enter the sea. As a risk, large-scale
pollution could cause significant environmental and financial damage, although the probability
of such an accident occurring is low according to the risk assessment procedure. Negative impacts
may also arise from the underwater cleaning and maintenance of vessels and the reception
of ship waste, during which pollutants, including microplastics, may enter the marine environment.
For Tallinna Sadam, excessive sewage from cruise ships and ferries in Old City Harbour also
constitutes a risk. Although this does not cause direct pollution of the marine environment, it may
lead to additional costs due to higher pollution fees applied by the water utility AS Tallinna Vesi.

In its activities, Tallinna Sadam is guided by environmental principles centred on the prevention
of pollution and environmental impacts. Tallinna Sadam's goal is to prevent the generation
of pollution and to protect the Baltic Sea as a sensitive ecosystem. The pollution prevention policy
is based on the principle that pollution must be prevented and avoided throughout the entire
operational process, relying on the avoidance of contamination.

With regard to atmospheric pollution, in addition to GHG (addressed as an IRO in section 6.2),
the air quality in the port area may be affected by dust generation during cargo loading, especially
when handling bulk peat. Unfavourable weather conditions (primarily strong winds) may cause dust
to spread across the port territory, which in turn affects the environment, residents of neighbouring
properties, and the activities of companies operating in the area. Risks related to dust are mitigated
by operators through continuous monitoring of handling activities and weather conditions, including
the suspension of work if environmental permit conditions are not met. However, dust prevention
may be limited, particularly in situations where the specific nature of handling processes or weather
conditions restrict dust suppression measures.

This principle is an integral part of Tallinna Sadam's sustainable development strategy and is
implemented through coordinated action in the development of port services and in cooperation
with shipping companies and other value chain participants.
Tallinna Sadam uses company-specific metrics to assess pollution impacts, which support the monitoring of strategic environmental targets and ensure transparent and purposeful reporting.

6.3.3 Air pollution
In addressing potential air pollution associated with Tallinna Sadam's activities, we have focused
on several key areas: monitoring GHG emission sources (section 6.2.5), odour issues arising from
cargo at oil terminals, air pollution from vessels, and the differentiation of vessel dues based on
the vessel's contribution to reducing air pollution. To achieve cleaner outdoor air, we have initiated
several projects and created innovative solutions in various fields.
To reduce traffic loads in the Old City Harbour area and improve the noise levels and air quality of
the region, more heavy-duty vehicles and other vehicles are being redirected from Old City Harbour
to Muuga Harbour and Paldiski South Harbour, where ro-pax vessels serve vehicles on the Paldiski–
Kapellskär and Muuga–Vuosaari routes. Double ramps are in use in both Old City Harbour and
Muuga Harbour, allowing for a significant increase in the speed of loading and unloading vehicles.
At the same time, faster loading and unloading reduces emissions from road traffic (including CO2).
The share of ro-ro units in Paldiski South Harbour and Muuga Harbour accounted for 37% of all
Tallinna Sadam ro-ro units in 2025 (2024: 36%).
Due to sanctions imposed on Russian and Belarusian goods and a significant decrease in the transit
of liquid fuels and petroleum products through Muuga Harbour, air monitoring at three stationary

monitoring stations has been suspended since 2024 by agreement with the Environmental Board;
as of 2025, air monitoring continues at only one monitoring station, Muuga-1. Necessary air pollution monitoring was also carried out using an electronic odour sensor (e-nose) monitoring system.
E-noses are positioned in the Muuga Harbour area for odour nuisance control, enabling the rapid identification of potential odour nuisances and the location of the source to resolve the issue
immediately. In 2025, no air pollution limit exceedances were recorded, and the port received no
complaints. The aforementioned air monitoring is carried out in cooperation with the oil operators
in Muuga Harbour.
We provide discounts on vessel dues for more environmentally friendly vessels based on the ESI10
index. In 2025, we provided discounts based on the ESI index for a total of 2,739 vessel calls,
accounting for 38% (2024: 38%) of the total number of vessel calls.
Additionally, we offer onshore power supply (OPS) and automated mooring services to liners
in Old City Harbour, which have helped reduce air pollution caused by vessels, as well as noise and
vibration. The Smart Port traffic management system has been developed in Muuga Harbour, Paldiski
South Harbour, and Old City Harbour, making the movement of vehicles onto vessels smooth and
fast, reducing unnecessary idling and the resulting emissions.

Water withdrawal and water consumption
Tallinna Sadam uses water in its own operations, for the functioning of harbour infrastructure, and
for sale to customers and vessels. In Muuga Harbour, water is withdrawn from three boreholes based
on an environmental permit issued by the Environmental Board, and the company complies with all
requirements and measures set out in the permit to ensure the sustainable use of water resources
and to prevent pollution. In Old City Harbour and Paldiski South Harbour, drinking water from the public
water supply system is used. The quantities of water withdrawn are determined based on measurement. For the sustainable use of water, water-saving devices are implemented, the condition
of pipelines is monitored, and regular maintenance of equipment is performed to reduce water
losses. To reduce the consumption of drinking water, an underground rainwater collection tank has
been installed in the area in front of Old City Harbour's D-terminal for watering plants. No water
was reused or stored during the reporting period.

Marine resources
To ensure that no sewage is discharged from vessels into the harbour waters, Tallinna Sadam
contributes to the reception of sewage, which is addressed in section 6.6. Sewage from the vessels
of TS Laevad, owned by Tallinna Sadam, is discharged into the collection tanks of AS Saarte Liinid's
harbours.
The management of ballast water from vessels is important for the protection of the marine environment, as invasive species and hazardous substances can enter the port through ballast water.
Ballast water management is carried out based on international requirements, and the discharge
of ballast water from visiting vessels into the port's harbour waters is restricted or prohibited.
Vessels may pump ballast water located in the segregated tanks of dry bulk carriers and tankers
overboard only if the ballast water meets the requirements set out in international conventions
and does not contain oil or other substances harmful to the environment.
We have also tightened other environmental restrictions for vessels. For example, underwater
hull cleaning services for vessels in Tallinna Sadam's harbour waters can only be provided on
the condition that cleaning waste is collected to prevent it from entering the marine environment.
The collection of waste from hull cleaning is necessary because the entry of any waste, as well
as algae, potential invasive species, microplastics, and nitrogen, phosphorus, and other nutrients
into the marine environment, contributes to the eutrophication of the already sensitive Baltic Sea
environment.
Monitoring of the marine environment associated with construction activities is addressed
in section 6.5.

6.5 E4 Biodiversity and ecosystems
Although biodiversity and ecosystems were not identified as a material topic as a result of Tallinna
Sadam's double materiality assessment, the following section addresses the monitoring of the state
of the marine environment and related activities to provide an overview of the potential impact of
port activities on the Baltic Sea ecosystem.
To assess the potential impacts of port activities, we monitor the species diversity of the sea
in the areas adjacent to the harbours. The long-term goal is that the number of species in the benthic communities near Old City Harbour (in Tallinn Bay) and Muuga Harbour does not differ from
the average values of the reference area (Kakumäe Bay). The selected metric reflects the potential
impact of port activities on the marine ecosystem, as the species composition of benthic communities is sensitive to changes in water transparency, the intensity of eutrophication, dredging, and other
stress factors.
In our assessment, we use data from the annual national environmental monitoring (Estonian Marine
Institute and the Environment Agency of the Ministry of Climate) concerning the number of benthic
species in Muuga Bay and Tallinn Bay. According to the 2025 monitoring data, the state of the sea
near Muuga Harbour was 87% (2024: 97%) and near Old City Harbour remained at the same level
as the reference area (100%).
With every activity, we monitor our impact on the natural environment, for which we carry out
pre-construction, during-construction, and post-construction marine environment monitoring.

The purpose of the monitoring is to assess the impact resulting from the port's activities and
the impact of filling and dredging works on the benthic fauna, flora, fish stock, and coastal processes
in the surrounding area.
During the period from 11 October 2025 to 15 November 2025, dredging works with a total volume
of 334,900 m3 were carried out in Paldiski South Harbour in connection with the construction
of quay 6A. During the dredging works, suspended solids monitoring was conducted in accordance
with the requirements of environmental permits No. KL-511943 and L.VV/325236. To monitor
the level and spread of suspended solids, spot measurements at monitoring stations, continuous
monitoring at buoy stations, and satellite monitoring were performed. The work was carried out by
the Marine Systems Institute of Tallinn University of Technology. The results indicate a short-term
and local impact caused by the dredging works, which was mainly limited to the dredged area and
the immediate vicinity of the harbour waterfront. Continuous monitoring at the buoy stations showed
that the level of suspended solids remained predominantly low during the dredging period. Satellite
monitoring, performed using Sentinel-2/MSI and Sentinel-3/OLCI satellite images with low cloud
cover, showed moderate spatial variation in suspended solids during the period preceding the dredging, which was consistent with prevailing wind conditions. On a cloud-free satellite image from
19 October 2025 taken during the dredging works, suspended solids concentrations in the surface
layer of the water were predominantly in the range of 5–10 mg/l, being higher in the immediate
vicinity of Paldiski South Harbour and in the near-shore areas of Pakri Bay. In the central part
of Pakri Bay, the level of suspended solids remained lower. A thorough environmental monitoring
report has been submitted to the Environmental Board.

6.6 E5 Resource use and circular economy
6.6.1 Material impacts, risks, and opportunities
As negative impacts, the implementation of circular economy solutions has been limited to date.
Ship waste is not diverted to recycling to a sufficient extent, which means that the reuse potential
of resources remains largely unrealised. If waste is handled incorrectly, part of it may enter
the environment.
In addition, significant consumption of non-renewable marine fuels occurs in the value chain,
which is one of the main sources of environmental impact (see section 6.2). Given the high number
of vessel calls and the associated energy demand, the impact is extensive.
As a positive impact, the company has a system in place through which all waste from vessels
is received. This creates a strong basis for its environmentally sound management and enables
the further diversion of waste into recycling, i.e. circulation or reuse.

6.6.2 Policies, measures, and targets
Tallinna Sadam follows the principles of the circular economy, which centre on the efficient use
of resources, the prevention of waste generation, and the promotion of reuse. The objective of
the company's circular economy policy is to reduce the environmental footprint by diverting waste
into recycling or reuse to the greatest extent possible and avoiding landfilling. The implementation
of the circular economy broadly supports the sustainable development strategy and contributes
to the achievement of climate goals.
The long-term goal is to divert at least 70% of waste collected from vessels into the circular
economy. Sufficient capacities are ensured in Tallinna Sadam's harbours for the reception of all
ship waste, including bilge water, oily residues, sewage, garbage, and scrubber waste.
In 2025, according to unaudited data, 58% of ship waste was diverted into recycling (2024: 55%).
The recycling rate is affected by both the composition of the waste by type and the capacity
of waste managers to process these materials. The share of landfilled waste is minimal — a total
of 98% of the received waste was diverted into recovery (including incineration) and recycling
(2024: 98%).

7.1.7 Occupational health and safety
Occupational safety is one of Tallinna Sadam's main priorities. Approximately 40% of the workforce
consists of office workers, whose main work environment risks are related to low mobility resulting
from forced positions and working with computers. The remaining 60% of employees are engaged
in high-risk work, such as working at heights or at night, as well as performing hot work, operating

to the risks of their field of work. Training includes, among others, training for working at heights, first
aid providers, fire safety, and forklift drivers, as well as training for work environment commissioners.
In addition, we use an e-learning environment that enables safety inductions and knowledge testing
to ensure better accessibility and reporting of training. A digitalised solution for issuing personal

With regard to workers in the value chain, health and safety, as well as training and skills development, were identified as primary positive impacts. Tallinna Sadam has established clear occupational
safety requirements for cooperation partners, arising from both contractual agreements and international norms and standards. In the shipping sector, safety practices exceeding legal minimum
requirements are applied in several cases, the impact of which extends across the entire value chain.
The implementation of these principles is supported by regular cooperation and supervision of partners, aimed at preventing occupational safety risks and ensuring a uniform safety culture throughout
the value chain.
In addition, joint exercises and training sessions are conducted in cooperation with partners and

knowledge of value chain workers in both shipping and harbour operations. Through training and
joint exercises, awareness of safety requirements is increased and the risk of occupational accidents
is reduced, thereby supporting both employee well-being and the quality of services throughout
the value chain.
Risks related to negative impacts on workers in the value chain are primarily associated with insufficient training of the cooperation partners' employees, which may result in occupational accidents
and subsequent financial loss. To mitigate this risk, Tallinna Sadam sets minimum requirements
for the competence and training of partners' employees and encourages participation in joint training
and exercises to improve safety awareness and readiness.
An additional risk identified was the lack of adequate wages in value chain companies, which may
lead to the partners' inability to ensure the necessary workforce and thus to the non-fulfilment
of contractual obligations. To manage this risk, Tallinna Sadam considers the ability of cooperation

partners to fulfil contractual obligations and assesses risks related to workforce sustainability when
selecting partners. If necessary, topics related to workforce and service continuity are addressed
in contractual terms and in cooperation with partners, with the aim of reducing the risk of service
interruptions and financial losses throughout the value chain.

Tallinna Sadam considers workers in the value chain an essential part of ensuring the quality, reliability, and safety of its services. Although value chain workers are not direct employees of Tallinna
Sadam, their working conditions, competence, and safety awareness directly affect the smoothness
of harbour operations and the ferry service, occupational safety, and the quality
of services provided to customers and end-users.
The central goal in managing the social impact related to value chain workers is to ensure that
agreed principles of occupational safety, health, and competence are followed throughout Tallinna
Sadam's value chain and that the risk of occupational accidents is minimised.
Targets:
• ensure a high level of occupational health and safety throughout the value chain;
• shape a common safety culture among cooperation partners and service providers;
• support the competence and readiness of value chain workers through training
and joint exercises;
• reduce the risk of occupational accidents, environmental and other incidents, service
interruptions, and associated financial and reputational damage.
The sustainable development goal is 0 serious occupational accidents and 0 serious incidents.

Tallinna Sadam monitors the risk of inadequate wages for value chain workers, although we lack realistic means to influence it. In cooperation agreements, we have stipulated minimum requirements
for the workforce and monitor their fulfilment.
The goal for the coming years is to develop and implement a set of business continuity measures
across the value chain in Tallinna Sadam's harbours for the export and import of strategic goods

The end-users of the Tallinna Sadam Group are passengers moving through the ports, port visitors,

related to access to services and information, as well as health, safety, and security, and high-quality
public space.

7.4.1 Material impacts, risks, and opportunities
With regard to end-users, access to services was identified as a doubly material positive impact.
Access to quality information, as well as health and safety and personal security when consuming
the service, were identified as material positive impacts.

In the field of health, safety, and personal security, a positive impact is manifested for end-users
through ensuring a safe and secure environment in the ports and port-related areas. Tallinna Sadam
pays attention to traffic safety, the safety of promenades and public areas, and seasonal risks, including snow and ice control. Security is also ensured in the Muuga free zone, Paldiski South Harbour and
the Old City Harbour area by implementing corresponding security measures and supervision. The aim
of these measures is to prevent accidents and ensure a safe service experience for end-users.

7.4.2 Policies, measures, and targets
The social dimension of Tallinna Sadam's sustainable development strategy is based on the principle
that, in addition to their economic role, ports are also important providers of public space, connectivity,
and security of supply. From the perspective of end-users, this primarily means ensuring the availability, safety, and security of services and a high-quality visitor experience.

A doubly material positive impact resulting from the activities of the Tallinna Sadam Group is manifested for end-users primarily through access to services, which plays a central role in the provision
of vital services. TS Laevad enables a regular shipping connection between the main islands and
the mainland, ensuring movement opportunities for island residents and visitors and uninterrupted
supply of goods necessary for life, including food, medicines, fuels, and other essential goods. Such
connectivity is of critical importance from the standpoint of the functioning of the regions, social
cohesion, and the well-being of the residents.

The objective related to end-users is to ensure the reliability and continuity of services, maintain
a high level in safety and security indicators, and provide a high-quality and accessible service that
strengthens the role of Tallinna Sadam and its subsidiaries as providers of reliable connectivity and
security of supply. The fulfilment of these objectives is assessed through service continuity, safety indicators, system reliability, and customer satisfaction.

In addition to vital services, Tallinna Sadam's activities support the import and export of strategic
goods, which takes place through the ports and is important for both the country's security of supply
and the functioning of the economy. Trade through the ports helps ensure the functioning of supply
chains and broadly supports the Estonian economy and security.

7.4.3 Access to services and information

A positive impact is also manifested in enabling the movement of people by sea, including through
passenger and ferry traffic. The ports create the prerequisite for international and domestic connectivity, supporting movement for work, tourism, and private purposes. Tallinna Sadam ensures access

shipping companies and other partners, aimed at ensuring the reliability and continuity of services

8.1.5 Risk management
In accordance with the risk management framework, we regularly monitor and assess the risks
affecting Tallinna Sadam's activities, which are divided into three main categories: external environment risks, business area risks, and operational risks. All risks are assigned risk severity levels (low,
medium, high or extreme) and mitigation measures, which depend on the nature of the specific risk.
An overview of the principles and methods of managing and mitigating the Group's financial risks
can be found in note 4 to the annual financial statements.
Among external environment risks, the Group's activities are most affected by the global unstable
economy, including potential overheating of the economy, rapid inflation, and economic crisis.
Risks related to the business activities of our major customers and the low readiness of potential
customers for investment can have a significant impact, as well as international and European-wide
restrictive environmental requirements and regulations (European Union Green Deal, including
the ‘Fit for 55’ climate package, decarbonisation of vessels, etc.), the impact of which is reflected
in the long term in potential changes in the demand for harbour services and the cost base. We also
assess potential energy supply interruptions caused by failures and/or energy deficits as a high risk
(though occurring rarely).
Great attention is paid to the management of information security risks and ensuring cybersecurity. Much work went into the implementation of the E-ITS information security standard, which is
mandatory for Tallinna Sadam. The E-ITS main audit held at the end of the year found that Tallinna
Sadam implements the Information Security Management System (ISMS) systematically and
at a good level. No findings with a high or critical risk level were found. DDoS attacks were constant in 2025, but their impact was marginal thanks to protection measures. The volume of emails
containing phishing and malware was very high. In addition, constant scanning of Tallinna Sadam's

systems was carried out from the network, which indicates that a consistent search for security
vulnerabilities is underway so that they can be exploited. Furthermore, information security training
is regularly organised for employees, and in 2025, the proportion of employees in the target group
who had completed it was 98% (2024: 77%).
The passenger business area is most affected by the decrease in ship calls due to the chartering
of passenger vessels to other markets because of high fuel prices and the lack of passengers,
as well as the increase in additional costs related to the implementation of regulations arising
from European climate policy in maritime transport, which in turn raises the price of the service
and reduces demand (especially in the cruise business) in the Baltic Sea region. In the cargo area,
the main risk is the restructuring of supply chains and logistics resulting from the loss of the eastern
market and general economic policy, due to which certain types of cargo no longer move through our
harbours. In the real estate area, significant risks are related to the potential delay of local government planning processes and potential litigation. In the shipping area, the greatest risks are related
to icebreaking services and offshore work, including the failure to find employment during the nonicebreaking period, potential technical problems of the Botnica, and the risk that the state will not
extend the contract for the provision of icebreaking services after 2032. Among operational risks,
significant risks are related to the potential decrease in the market value of subsidiaries and significant assets. Other risks related to Tallinna Sadam's activities, including environmental, safety and
security, compliance, and personnel-related risks, are estimated to remain at either a medium or low
level (considering risk mitigation measures).
Climate-related physical and transition risks are addressed separately in the climate risk analysis
(see section 6.1), which was conducted based on the TCFD framework and the results of which show
that the impact of climate risks on Tallinna Sadam's activities and assets is assessed as low across
all time horizons, considering existing mitigation and prevention measures.

8.1.9 Payment practices and tax conduct
In its business activities, Tallinna Sadam follows good payment practices and considers timely and
correct settlement with its suppliers important. Invoices are paid to suppliers within the deadlines
agreed in contracts or on invoices, and unjustified payment delays are avoided. Payments are made
based on the principles in force in the company, and suppliers are treated equally, regardless
of their size.
Tallinna Sadam is a responsible taxpayer and follows tax-related legislation and good tax conduct
practices in its activities. The company pays taxes by the deadlines specified in the legislation and
submits tax reports in a timely and correct manner. Tallinna Sadam does not engage in transactions
aimed at creating tax benefits or avoiding the payment of taxes based on tax legislation. The company operates in an international business environment and is guided by OECD tax guidelines in its
activities. If questions concerning taxation arise, Tallinna Sadam consults with the Tax and Customs
Board as necessary. In the event of taxation issues arising from green tax legislation, the company
involves tax auditors and tax law compliance experts in the resolution to ensure that tax accounting
complies with current requirements.
