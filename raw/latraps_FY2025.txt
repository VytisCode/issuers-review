Cooperative “LATRAPS” (hereinafter –
LATRAPS) presents information on the company’s
most significant environmental, social, and
governance (ESG) indicators. The report covers
LATRAPS ’ operations at its main site in Eleja, as

Group’s overall sustainability strategy, including its

sustainability, a double materiality assessment
was conducted in 2025. The assessment
evaluated both the company’s impacts on the
environment and the influence of external
factors on the company, particularly in terms of
risks and opportunities. In preparing this
Sustainability Report, industry best practices
and the requirements of the European
Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS)
were taken into account, whereas the structure
of the sustainability disclosures was developed
in accordance with the Voluntary Sustainability
Reporting Standard for SMEs (VSME). The
Report covers information on the company’s
activities for the financial year from 1 July 2025
to 30 June 2026.

Strategy: business model and sustainability-related initiatives (C1)
Strategy, business model and value chain

Value chain and cooperative approach

LATRAPS is the largest agricultural cooperative in the Baltics. Its core business is to provide
comprehensive services to farmers, covering the entire grain production and marketing cycle.
LATRAPS provides crop insurance agency services, as well as sales of fuel, agricultural
machinery and spare parts. It also offers equipment maintenance and logistics services and is
engaged in the production of rapeseed oil.

LATRAPS’ value chain is based on the principles of cooperation, with the aim of creating an
efficient, regionally integrated and resource-efficient agricultural system. The cooperative
approach involves the centralised development of infrastructure to reduce unnecessary transport
movements, optimise the use of resources and minimise environmental impacts.

LATRAPS is a 100% domestic capital company, owned by 1,214
Latvian farmers who are members of the cooperative.
The cooperative’s activities are driven by the needs of its members, bringing them
together to facilitate more efficient trading and improve the economic profitability of their
farming operations. It represents and supports the interests of its members by marketing
their agricultural produce, including grain, legumes and rapeseed, and supplying the
inputs and services required for agricultural production. The cooperative also provides
expert advice and organises training to support sustainable growth.

LATRAPS members operate farms with an average area of 332 hectares and
collectively manage approximately 30% of Latvia’s agricultural land.

During the reporting year, the company employed an average of 223 people.

The LATRAPS cooperative model, which brings together more than 1,214 members, is built
around two key benefits: jointly developed infrastructure and a centralised procurement and sales
system. Grain primary processing facilities established across the regions enable members to
organise logistics more efficiently, reduce transport flows and make use of shared, energy-efficient
infrastructure. This eliminates the need for individual farms to invest separately in their own grain
dryers and storage facilities.
The cooperative approach also supports a regionally organised system in which grain is not
transported immediately to ports or over long distances but is first processed closer to where it is
grown. This helps reduce transport intensity, fuel consumption and logistics-related emissions.
At the same time, we are developing energy-efficient facilities and assessing alternative solutions,
including mobile grain dryers and more efficient energy sources for drying processes. In addition,
the centralised procurement and sales system provides significant economic benefits. For individual
farmers, purchasing ingredients and identifying the most favourable markets for selling grain would
be considerably more costly and challenging. The cooperative model enables members to pool their
production, consolidate grain volumes and enter into larger transactions that would often be
inaccessible to individual farmers. This model strengthens members’ competitiveness, reduces
costs and, at the same time, promotes more sustainable and resource-efficient farming practices.

Market:
LATRAPS continues establishing partnership with major international export partners, including
global agricultural trading companies, to facilitate the sale of its members’ produce in international
markets and reduce counterparty risks.
In Latvia, LATRAPS cooperates with companies operating in the food production, energy
(biodiesel production) and livestock sectors.
In the 2025/2026 financial year, the domestic market accounted for 68% of LATRAPS’
revenue, while export markets accounted for 39%.

Practices, policies and future initiatives for the transition to a more sustainable economy (B2)
Sustainability direction
LATRAPS’ sustainability development direction
LATRAPS is building a sustainable, competitive and future-oriented agricultural ecosystem in which economic growth, environmental protection and social responsibility are closely interconnected,
while good governance strengthens the cooperative model as a foundation for sustainable growth.
Cooperation
The cooperative model is at the heart of LATRAPS operations, providing its members with equal opportunities to engage with the global market. Bringing farmers together within a unified system
strengthens their market position, facilitates knowledge sharing and creates greater resilience to external economic and climate-related risks.
Environmental impact
LATRAPS invests in energy efficiency and renewable energy
sources, while improving its processes and infrastructure to
reduce the consumption of fossil resources in grain drying,
logistics and other operations.
LATRAPS actively cooperates with scientific institutions and
researchers to develop more environmentally friendly solutions
for agriculture and promote more efficient use of resources.
LATRAPS educates its members and develops IT tools that
support data- and research-based decision-making, helping to
reduce environmental impacts while improving production
efficiency.
LATRAPS invests in sustainability by promoting product
diversification, including the development of legumes and
alternative protein sources. These initiatives support the
transition towards a more sustainable food system while
creating new economic opportunities for farmers.

Social impact

Governance

LATRAPS maintains an inclusive and safe working
environment that fosters a passionate, fully-engaged and
self-motivated team of employees.

LATRAPS ensures transparent, inclusive and responsible
decision-making, strengthening trust and long-term
cooperation among its members.

LATRAPS core values are cooperation, trust and mutual
responsibility. These values are reflected in the continuously
growing number of members and their active involvement in the
cooperative’s activities.

LATRAPS working environment is based on clear internal
procedures, equal treatment, non-discrimination, good corporate
governance and fair relationships among all stakeholders.

LATRAPS is a continuously learning organisation. It promotes
knowledge sharing, organises training and strengthens the
competencies of employees and members, while supporting the
development of local communities. In doing so, the company helps
create a stable foundation for sustainable agriculture in Latvia.

LATRAPS has developed a corporate development strategy up until the 2028/2029 financial year, with the majority of its strategic objectives linked to aspects that are material to the company’s
sustainability. Material sustainability matters are integrated into day-to-day operations. Sustainability is not a separate area of the company’s operations, but rather an approach in which impacts, risks and
opportunities are considered in every decision - from the working environment, product development and innovation to cooperation with members and partners.

Results of the double materiality assessment

HIGH

Double materiality assessment matrix
During the 2024/2025 reporting year, LATRAPS
conducted a double materiality assessment using
the European Sustainability Reporting Standards
(ESRS) methodology. The assessment analysed
environmental, social and governance aspects
across the company’s entire value chain, taking into
account the specific nature of the company’s
operations, industry and market trends, as well as
internally and externally identified impacts, risks
and opportunities (IROs). These factors were
grouped according to the ESRS topics and subtopics, with an overall score determined for each
sub-topic based on impact materiality and financial
materiality. The materiality threshold for both impact
materiality and financial materiality (risks and
opportunities) was set at 3 points on a 5-point
scale. Accordingly, topics with an overall score of at
least 3 points were considered material in the
context of LATRAPS sustainability reporting. In
addition, the Executive Board decided to include in
the list of material sustainability topics those
identified by experts, including occupational health
and safety, safety-related impact, climate change
adaptation and product quality, in order to fully
reflect the views of stakeholders. As a result, the
material topics relevant to LATRAPS strategic
development, risk management and sustainability
priorities were identified, providing a basis for the
further development of objectives, actions and
performance indicators. The assessment results
are presented in a double materiality matrix, which
visually illustrates the priority aspects based on
their impact materiality and financial materiality and
provides the basis for the development of the

Transport

SUSTAINABILITY REPORT

Environmental
impact

Transport

Fleet electrification

LATRAPS provides grain and mineral fertiliser logistics using both its own fleet of up to 10 trucks
and external transport service providers, operating an average of approximately 35 vehicles per
year. At the same time, the company is developing rail transportation of grain as one of its priority
sustainable logistics solutions. During the 2024/2025 reporting year, more than 600,000 tonnes of
grain and mineral fertilisers were transported in total. The volume of rail transportation was 18.8%
higher than the volume transported by road. This approach helps reduce greenhouse gas
emissions from transportation, as rail transport generates significantly lower CO₂ emissions per
tonne transported compared with road transport.

LATRAPS is gradually electrifying its vehicle fleet. This initiative forms part of a broader energy
transition aimed at reducing fossil fuel consumption and shifting towards more sustainable energy
solutions. Over the next five years, the company plans to advance fleet electrification in several
areas, including the transition to electric vehicles for office-related needs, assessing opportunities
to introduce electric commercial vehicles, and developing electric vehicle charging infrastructure at
the company’s facilities. LATRAPS already uses electric equipment in its day-to-day operations.
The company currently has six electric pallet trucks and forklifts, which are used in warehouse and
logistics processes.

Global developments are increasingly affecting the long-term competitiveness of Latvian
agricultural products. Fluctuations in energy prices, rising transport infrastructure costs and the
restructuring of supply chains are putting pressure on transportation costs across the region.
Given that rail transportation costs increased by approximately 12% on average over the past
year, the use of rail transport for grain shipments may decline during the next operating period.
To provide more competitive transportation solutions, LATRAPS has developed the digital
platform STROPS, which connects owners of agricultural cargo with transport providers.
The platform helps optimise regional transportation by offering more flexible and potentially
more cost-effective transport alternatives. In 2026, the platform will be expanded with new
functionality enabling farms to submit cargo transportation requests and receive the most
competitive price offers.

Pollution of air, water and soil (B4)

SUSTAINABILITY REPORT

Environmental
impact

Air pollution measurements are carried out only when required by the relevant regulatory authorities.
During the reporting period, no complaints related to air pollution caused by the company's activities
were received, and no violations were identified during inspections conducted by the competent
authorities. This confirms the company's compliance with applicable regulatory requirements and
effective environmental management. Given the nature of the company's operations, its impact on
water and soil pollution is assessed as limited. The company ensures compliance with applicable
environmental requirements in these areas as well. For example, wastewater treatment is carried out
at the malt facility, and pollution monitoring is conducted twice a year.

Pollution of air, water and soil (B4)
Air pollution
Policies and approach

The most significant pollution-related environmental aspect of LATRAPS operations is air
pollution, which mainly arises from grain primary processing activities, particularly during the
drying process. These processes are energy-intensive and associated with emissions.
Therefore, the company identifies air pollution as a significant area of environmental impact
within its operations.

Circulation and use of mineral fertilisers
and plant protection products
The substances used in LATRAPS' operations that are of concern or of high concern are primarily
associated with the circulation and use of mineral fertilisers and plant protection products.
These substances are identified as a significant environmental aspect with a high impact potential.
The use of these substances may have adverse effects on soil and water, affecting ecosystem
balance and potentially human health through the food chain.

Action area

To ensure compliance with air quality standards and the requirements of permits for polluting
activities.

In the long term, inappropriate or excessive use of these substances may reduce soil fertility,
contribute to the development of pest resistance, negatively affect crop yield stability, and impact
the surrounding environment, including groundwater, local water bodies, biodiversity and human
health.

Actions and measures

In 2023, the average use of pesticides in Europe was approximately 1.6 kg/ha, slightly above the
global average. In Latvia, pesticide use for certain crops, such as grain, is lower, at approximately 1.1
kg/ha, indicating moderate pesticide use.

The company ensures compliance with applicable regulatory requirements by adhering to the
conditions of the B-category permits for polluting activities, which establish requirements for
emission limits, monitoring and management.

Policies and approach

In 2025, B-category permits were granted for the Aizkraukle, Jekabpils, Bauska and Eleja
facilities, with the Jaunpagasts facility covered from 2026.
In accordance with these permits, LATRAPS regularly monitors air pollution, ensures control
of technological processes and implements the necessary measures to reduce emissions.

Given LATRAPS role in the agricultural supply chain and its cooperation with farmers, responsible,
management and use of these substances is essential to reducing environmental risks and
supporting sustainable agricultural development. LATRAPS' approach is to use plant protection
products and mineral fertilisers efficiently in agricultural processes, promoting economically justified
and responsible use.

The company systematically evaluates opportunities to improve technological processes and
reduce air pollution, including by optimising the use of energy resources and introducing more
efficient equipment. Air pollution management is integrated into the company's operational
processes and forms part of its broader approach to sustainable and responsible business
operations.

• Early sowing of wheat makes it possible to significantly

Peas are our future – Limited Liability company SIA ASNS Ingredient

SUSTAINABILITY REPORT

Peas are our future – Limited Liability
company SIA ASNS Ingredient
The Limited Liability Company ASNS Ingredient is a strategically significant project of the
LATRAPS Group , marking an important transformation in Latvian agriculture – a shift from
being an exporter of primary agricultural products to becoming a producer of high value-added
food ingredients. Legumes, particularly peas, are crops with strong future potential and at the
same time contributing to sustainable agriculture, the achievement of climate targets and the
development of new market opportunities.
The new pea protein isolate plant being built in Jelgava will be the largest of its kind in
Northern Europe and, thanks to its advanced technological solutions, will also be among the
most modern facilities in the world, ensuring high levels of efficiency and productivity. Total
investment will amount to EUR 132 million. Construction is progressing according to schedule
and is expected to be completed by the end of 2026, with the plant scheduled to open in early
2027. The facility will occupy an approximately 10-hectare site and will process up to 70,000
tonnes of peas annually.
The primary objective of ASNS Ingredient is to establish a complete value chain – from field to
processing – using peas grown by Latvian farmers as the main raw material. Alongside the
development of the plant, LATRAPS is actively working to identify high-yielding, lodging-resistant
pea varieties with high protein content and to further develop pea cultivation technologies.
The plant will produce several high value-added products:
• Pea protein isolate produced using a wet extraction process to ensure high solubility and a
neutral taste
• Modified protein with enhanced water-binding, gelling and emulsification properties
• Pea starch with a high amylose content and pronounced gelling properties
• Pea fibre produced through an extrusion process and suitable for structuring plant-based food
products
These products have a wide range of applications across the food, medical and agricultural
sectors, particularly in the plant-based food segment, where global demand is growing rapidly.
The project is technologically complex and also includes the development of associated
infrastructure, including energy supply, heat supply, water treatment and biogas production
solutions. Some of these solutions will be developed in cooperation with external partners and
may potentially be offered to the market as standalone services.

Energy production and emissions reduction
The plant’s energy supply system is designed to use a wood-chip boiler house,
replacing fossil fuels for heat generation. Planned annual energy production is
approximately 130,000 MWh. Compared with the use of natural gas, this solution is
expected to reduce CO₂ emissions by approximately 24,170 tonnes per year.
At the same time, the company is considering various additional renewable energy
solutions. The plant’s design retains the option to develop solar energy infrastructure in
the future, including the installation of solar panels on building rooftops.

Biogas and water treatment solutions
The plant’s technological solutions are being developed with the aim of moving towards
a closed-loop model, in which process by-products are reused and returned to the
production cycle. Following appropriate chemical analyses, the aerobic sludge
generated during the process water treatment and ash from the boiler house are
expected to be suitable for agricultural use, returning nutrients to the soil.
Anaerobic sludge, meanwhile, has the potential to become a commercially viable
product. The evaluation of technological solutions is currently ongoing, including further
analysis of process water composition and clarification of data on potential byproducts. Consequently, comprehensive impact assessments are still being developed.

By-product management
The outer pea hulls and fibre fractions generated during pea processing are
planned to be sold on the market, whereas damaged peas and mechanical
impurities are intended to be used for energy production together with wood
chips.

Energy efficiency solutions

It is expected to implement several energy-efficiency solutions in the plant,
including an absorption heat pump for cooling generation. This technology is still
relatively uncommon in Latvia, but it enables more efficient use of thermal energy
and reduces electricity consumption.

SUSTAINABILITY REPORT

Latvia’s malt producer – LATMALT
More than ten years ago, the malt house SIA LATMALT (hereinafter – LATMALT ) was
established in Stalgene village. It is currently the only company in Latvia producing locally
sourced barley, rye and wheat malt. The company has made a significant contribution to the
development of local food and beer production, created new jobs in the region and strengthened
the added value of Latvian agriculture.
LATMALT’s operations are based on the careful selection and processing of high-quality grain
into malt. The production process begins with the selection and soaking of suitable grains,
followed by germination, during which natural enzymes are activated. The malt is then dried and
kilned, giving each product its characteristic taste, aroma and colour. The finished malt is used in
beer brewing and baking of bread, making it an important ingredient in Latvia’s food and beverage
industries.
LATMALT offers a wide range of malt products:
• light barley malt with a sweet taste and clean malt aroma
• Munich malt – a medium-roasted caramel malt with a pronounced balance of sweetness and
bitterness
• wheat malt, suitable for producing top-fermented beers with a pronounced wheat aroma
• dark barley malts with a richer flavour profile
• rye malt with a pronounced rye bread aroma and high enzyme activity
The company’s flexible production technologies allow the properties of malt to be tailored to
customers’ needs and enable the production of both small and large batches. LATMALT products
are used by breweries and bakeries in Latvia, providing locally sourced, high-quality ingredients.
For the LATMALT team, malt production and entrepreneurship are not merely a business but
rather a long-term investment in the future, the prosperity of future generations of Latvians, and a
safe and well-managed environment. The company implements sustainability-promoting measures
and practices in its day-to-day operations, including the following areas:

Energy efficiency and CO₂ emissions reduction

Malt production is energy-intensive, so the company continuously seeks solutions to
reduce its CO₂ footprint through the use of renewable energy sources. To support this
goal, a solar farm has been built, generating an average of 120 MWh of electricity per
year and covering 18.5% of the plant’s total electricity consumption.

Responsible resource use and circular economy practices
The malting process requires significant volumes of water resources; therefore, the
company aims to reduce water consumption, for example, by increasing production
automation, which helps minimise water use in production and sanitation activities, and
also ensuring that water is discharged back into the environment in compliance with the
quality requirements set out in the Cabinet Regulations.

Sludge generated during the wastewater treatment process, along with other organic
waste, is used for biogas production. By-products generated during the cleaning of
grain and malt are sold to third parties. During grain germination, another production
by-product is generated
– sprouts and rootlets, which, due to their high protein content, are used as a
valuable feed product and sold to third parties for use as animal feed.

Social impact

The company ensures compliance with labour rights, employment and occupational
health and safety requirements. Remuneration is determined according to the
position and qualifications of employees, with the company proactively ensuring that
wages exceed the statutory minimum wage.
The company has implemented an occupational health and safety management
system. Risk assessments are carried out regularly, and employee training and
emergency response drills are organised.
No cases of child labour, forced labour or discrimination have been identified within the
company.

Good governance and ethical principles
The company has implemented ethics and disciplinary policies, including an employee
grievance procedure that ensures confidentiality and protection against potential
mobbing or bossing.

Over the past three years, the company has not received any fines or
warnings for non-compliance with environmental requirements.
